Weekly Lab Notes (Nov 24th). When you move countries, you don't just pack boxes. You drag nearly 30 years of digital life along with you. Mine was a mess.

I started with this:

Several terabytes of old files from generations of computers I backed up onto external SSD, and HDD going back to 1997, and hastily uploaded to iCloud over the years with minimal pruning.

106,000+ photos and videos just in iPhoto, and many more in older photo libraries in old backups.

130,000+ emails going back to 2004.

Chat logs going back to 2007.

So I gave my Mac assistant this mess over about three weeks, and what came out of it was clean, organized, and easy to find anything from my 30 years of digital file collecting, with most of the junk cleared out.

iCloud became 133 GB of organised files, mostly by nuking duplicates, compressing source trees into ZIP files, and jettisoning off lots of junk that came along with backup software from ages ago.

Google Drive became 647 files sorted, and 33 forgotten "anyone with the link" shares closed.

Gmail went from 131,716 messages to about 43,000, with more than 88,000 stale, old, or junk emails deleted. Inside the backup were 1,829 chat logs from 2007 to 2013 I'd forgotten existed!

With my Photos, 106,678 items became about 40,600. The old library had been imported three times, and some 2012 videos existed in five copies of up to 4 GB each.

Now every file sits in one catalog. Search finds any of them by name, and reads inside my personal papers, scans included, in English and Spanish. A search takes under a tenth of a second.

The payoff moment: I needed a signed contract and a year of statements from an old vendor. They were open in under a minute.

Every Sunday at 10, a sweep emails me a plan for whatever new documents or downloads piled up on both my Macs that week. Nothing moves until I reply "approve".

The rule I set at the beginning: the AI will never permanently delete anything. Backups come first, and every move is journalled. That's more than 13,500 moves so far, and every one can be undone.

The rules earned their keep. One click trashed 20 original photos along with 62,901 copies. The plan file restored all of them, then removed only the copies. Nothing was lost.

My favourite bug: I was sure two of my Macs were fighting each other. They weren't: Apple's servers kept bringing back folders I'd deleted, about 885,000 records' worth.
