Lab Notes (November 10th). The hardest lesson of moving abroad had nothing to do with Spain. It cost my family nearly $18,000 and happened with our home in Seattle.

When we left the US, we decided to rent out our house through a small, local property management firm. Then the rent stopped arriving. After they missed paying one month, then another, you bet we got a little worried.  We found the renters were still paying, but the PM firm simply stopped paying its bills.

I found out I wasn't alone. Through Google and Yelp reviews, owners started finding each other.  In the end more than 100 owners, roughly 130 properties, and more than $1.4 million in unpaid rent, reserves and deposits from this one property management firm going dark.  But there was no organization, nothing to turn our collective set of bad situations into an organized voice of action.

From nine time zones away I also had to find a new property manager. We had briefly talked about just selling the house, but we then got rational again and interviewed and hired a larger property management company to take over for the failed one.  And you bet we vetted five times harder than we did initially. 

Then I did what builders do: I built.  And as a leader, organized people to take action.

In August a public site for the affected owners went live, the same day as its first commit.  Easy enough to do these days.

Then came a private portal: an intake form to verified affected owners, an organiser vetting queue, digests and guides, and tools for affected owners.  Plus a Whatsapp chat to discuss how we could help all these other perfect strangers in a bad situation find solutions.

With written guides, and effective leadership, all these owners coordinated filing with the state licensing department, the Attorney General, Seattle Police and the FBI's internet crime centre. Pro se Civil suits followed in King County, all without an attorney charging $800 an hour.

A local paper (the West Seattle Blog) ran an in-depth story.

Today, the organisers wrote to 23 Washington officials (incumbent and newly elected) asking for five changes to state law. Any owner can now write to their own officials in under 10 minutes through the owners portal I built.

I'm in Jerez de la Frontera, Spain. Three fellow volunteer organisers are in Seattle. 72 of the 130 owners help each other in one group chat, so nobody is working for anyone else, we are just helping each other with a pretty terrible situation.

No court or agency has decided any claim yet, justice takes time. But a hundred scattered owners became one organised voice through these efforts, tools, and a focused call to action.

Thirty years of leading teams taught me how to do that. Semi-retirement didn't take it away. It just changed who and how I lead, when the opportunity arises.

Still building.  Still leading :)
