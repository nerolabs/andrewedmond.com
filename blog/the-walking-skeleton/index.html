The walking skeleton

Ugly, seven obligations, nothing you’d screenshot — but connected end to end on day one. The oldest discipline I know, and the one AI makes more important, not less.

Jul 11, 2026·2 min read

The first version of Get Camino would have failed every design review I’ve ever run.

Seven obligations in the catalog. A barely-styled plan page. Nothing you’d screenshot for LinkedIn.

But it did the one thing no beautiful demo does: it ran end-to-end. Interview → profile → deterministic engine → personalized plan, all connected, on day one. A walking skeleton.

This old discipline matters more with AI, not less. An AI coding agent will happily polish disconnected pieces forever — gorgeous screens with no data behind them, a clever engine no interface can reach. Ask for impressive and you’ll get impressive, in fragments. Demos are cheap now. Integration is where the truth lives, and a skeleton forces every seam into the open in week one instead of week six.

The payoff compounded. Every milestone after the first — the 60-obligation catalog, Lola the AI guide, localization, the mobile apps — was an organ transplanted into a living body, not a part bolted onto a pile. When something broke, it broke inside a running system that told us where.

Was day one perfect? Claude image-searched a hero photo of Spain for the landing page — and confidently shipped a picture of a kitten. Labeled “Madrid.”

The typing was free. Noticing the cat was my job. That’s the whole series in one bug.

The question for an AI-accelerated team isn’t “what have you built?” It’s “what runs end-to-end today?”
